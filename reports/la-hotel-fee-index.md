# LA Hotel Fee Index, October 2026: median stated self-parking charge is $28.50 a night

The Journeyed LA Hotel Fee Index. Published 2026-10-14. Data checked 2026-10-21.

A monthly index of the fees hotels in the Los Angeles area state on their own websites. October 2026 edition: 213 hotels state a self-parking price and 155 (73%) say it is free, with a median charge of $28.50 a night where one is charged. 27 hotels state a resort or destination fee amount, median $40 a night. 46 state a pet fee, median $100.

Sample: 277 hotels that state at least one fee, out of 451 hotel websites with a detail read and 2,608 hotels in the market.

Edition: October 2026 (data checked through October 21, 2026). Next edition: first business Tuesday of November 2026.

## Headline numbers

- **$28.50** median stated self-parking charge per night, where one is charged (48 hotels stating a charge, from $10 to $79)
- **73%** of hotels that state a self-parking price say it is free (155 of 213 hotels stating a self-parking price)
- **$40** median stated resort or destination fee per night (27 hotels stating an amount, from $3 to $125)
- **7%** of hotel websites read state a resort fee policy, or that there is none (31 of 451 hotel websites with a detail read)
- **$100** median stated pet fee (46 hotels stating a fee, from $25 to $350)
- **$60.50** median stated valet charge per night (29 hotels stating a charge, from $25 to $79)

## What do LA hotels charge in fees?

One table, every fee the index tracks. Each row counts the hotels in the Los Angeles area whose own website states that fee, how many say it is free or that there is none, how many state an amount, and the median and range of those amounts.

California's SB 478 took effect on July 1, 2024 and makes it unlawful to advertise a price that leaves out mandatory fees other than government taxes.

The FTC's Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees took effect on May 12, 2025 and requires short-term lodging prices to be advertised with mandatory fees included.

## How we got these numbers

What counts as a fee: a charge the hotel states on its own website for self-parking, valet parking, a resort, destination or amenity fee, a pet, smoking in a room, a security deposit or card hold, early check-in or late check-out. A hotel that states the charge is free, or that there is none, is counted as stating a policy. A hotel whose website says nothing is not counted either way.

Which hotels: every hotel, motel, inn and hostel published for the Los Angeles area (2,608 this edition). Only a hotel whose own website states a fee enters a figure; no booking site, review site or guest report is read. Each fact is read within the website's robots.txt and carries its source URL and the date it was checked in the CSV.

How medians are taken: one value per hotel, the latest the crawler read, with "free" and "none" counted as a policy but not as an amount. A median is shown only with at least 5 stated amounts and an area only with at least 5 hotels stating the fee; below that the cell says "too few to report". Amounts are per night as the hotel states them, except pet fees, which are per stay or per night as stated.

Editions: the index is published monthly, on the first business Tuesday, from the facts checked through the date shown. Each edition's rows are kept in the CSV under its edition column, so this URL accumulates the series.

Every number on this page is computed from facts that Journeyed read on hotels' own websites, within each site's robots.txt, between October 2, 2026 and October 21, 2026. Nothing comes from booking sites, review sites or guest reports.

The Los Angeles market on this site covers Los Angeles County and most of Orange County, from Malibu and Lancaster to Anaheim, Newport Beach and Laguna Beach, plus Avalon on Catalina Island. It has 2,608 hotels, motels, inns and hostels. 2,224 of them list their own website, and we have read at least one detail about the stay from 451 of those websites so far. A website our crawler opened but found no such detail on is not among the 451, so a share of "websites with a detail read" is higher than the share of all hotel websites would be.

A hotel is counted only when its website states the fact in plain text. Our crawler reads up to 12 pages per site and runs no JavaScript, so a fee that appears only inside a booking widget, an image or a PDF is not counted. A hotel that is not counted may still offer or charge the thing; it has not said so in a form we could read.

Shares are of the hotels that state the fact, never of all hotels, and each share shows its count. Hotels with good news, such as free parking or no resort fee, may be more likely to say so, which would make these shares look better than the whole market.

Values are read by software and can be wrong. Before publishing, a person re-read the highest and lowest values against the hotel's page. Hotels are named in a table of highest stated prices only when that exact value was re-read by hand. Every row in the CSV links to the page it was read from, so anyone can check it.

A median is shown only with at least 5 stated prices and an area only with at least 5 hotels stating the fact. The numbers are rebuilt with the site by published, deterministic code: the same fact rows always give the same numbers.
